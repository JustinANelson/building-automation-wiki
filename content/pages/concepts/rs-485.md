## Overview and Physical Signaling

**TIA/EIA-485** (commonly termed **RS-485**) is the foundational physical layer standard for local sub-networks in building automation systems (BAS). Unlike single-ended interfaces such as RS-232 that measure a voltage relative to a local chassis ground, RS-485 transmits digital data as a **differential voltage** across a balanced twisted pair of conductors (designated `A` and `B` or `-` and `+`):

$$\Delta V = V_{B} - V_{A} = V_{D1} - V_{D0}$$

The receiver evaluates the polarity of the differential voltage:
- **Binary 1 (Mark / Idle / OFF)**: Transceiver terminal $B$ is positive relative to $A$ ($\Delta V \ge +200\text{ mV}$).
- **Binary 0 (Space / Active / ON)**: Transceiver terminal $A$ is positive relative to $B$ ($\Delta V \le -200\text{ mV}$).
- **Undefined / Transition Region**: The deadband between $-200\text{ mV}$ and $+200\text{ mV}$.

Because electromagnetic interference (EMI) from variable frequency drives, elevator motors, and ballast transformers couples equally into both conductors of the tightly twisted pair, common-mode noise does not change the differential voltage between conductors, giving RS-485 exceptional noise immunity over long industrial runs.

## Electrical Characteristics and Limits

Per the TIA/EIA-485 specification and the Modbus Serial Line Implementation Guide V1.02:

- **Baud Rates**: Common BAS field bus rates include `9,600`, `19,200`, `38,400`, `57,600`, `76,800`, and `115,200 bps`.
- **Cable Specifications**: Low-capacitance twisted-pair shielded cable (typically `22 AWG` or `24 AWG` stranded copper) with characteristic impedance $Z_0 \approx 100\text{–}120\ \Omega$ and wire-to-wire capacitance $< 50\text{ pF/m}$ ($< 15\text{ pF/ft}$).
- **Common-Mode Voltage Window**: Transceivers must tolerate a common-mode ground potential difference between $-7\text{ VDC}$ and $+12\text{ VDC}$ relative to the driver ground without signal corruption or component damage.
- **Maximum Trunk Length**: Up to `4,000 ft` (`1,200 m`) at baud rates up to `76.8 kbps` (`1,000 m` at `9,600 bps` on `26 AWG`). Longer runs require optically isolated repeaters.
- **Unit Load (`UL`) Capacities**: The original standard defines a standard unit load as an input impedance of $\approx 12\text{ k}\Omega$, permitting a maximum of `32` full unit loads per segment. Modern transceivers use fractional loads (`1/4 UL` or `1/8 UL`), allowing `64` to `128` physical controllers on a single segment without repeaters.

## Topology and Line Installation Rules

Reliable RS-485 communication requires strict compliance with four physical installation rules:

1. **Continuous Daisy-Chain Trunk**: Controllers must be wired in a strict point-to-point "in-and-out" daisy chain. Star, ring, tree, or tee taps create impedance mismatches that reflect transmission edges back into the bus, corrupting UART framing bits.
2. **End-of-Line (`EOL`) Termination**: A $120\ \Omega$ ($1/4\text{ W}$, $1\%$) resistor must be placed across terminals $A$ and $B$ at the **two extreme physical ends** of the trunk cable (and nowhere else). The termination resistor matches the characteristic impedance of the transmission cable ($Z_0 \approx 120\ \Omega$), preventing signal reflections from open ends.
3. **Fail-Safe Line Polarization (Biasing)**: When no transceiver is actively transmitting (such as token turnover between controllers on a BACnet MS/TP trunk), the bus floats into high-impedance tri-state mode. A polarization network—a pull-up resistor (typically `470–560 Ω`) to $+5\text{ VDC}$ on terminal $B$ and a pull-down resistor to Ground on terminal $A$—forces a continuous bias voltage ($> +200\text{ mV}$) across the idle lines, preventing floating line noise from triggering phantom start bits.
4. **Shield Grounding and 3-Wire Signal Reference**: While often described as "2-wire", robust field buses require a reference conductor. The shield drain wire must be spliced continuously through all controller locations and connected to **earth ground at one single point only** (typically at the supervisory panel or power supply). Grounding the shield at both ends introduces circulating ground loops driven by building electrical potential differences.

## Common Protocol Deployments in BAS

- **BACnet MS/TP (Master-Slave/Token-Passing)**: ANSI/ASHRAE Standard 135 field bus operating at `9.6`, `19.2`, `38.4`, or `76.8 kbps` token-passing between peers.
- **Modbus RTU**: Modbus Organization serial master/slave communication standard using 8-bit binary framing with CRC-16 error checking.
- **Siemens APOGEE Floor Level Network (P1 FLN)**: Siemens proprietary RS-485 master-subordinate field bus between terminal equipment controllers (PTEC/ATEC) and field panels (MBC/MEC/PXC). The Siemens PXC Compact and Modular data sheets specify the P1 FLN port as `4800 bps to 38.4 Kbps` at 1/8 unit load; see [APOGEE FLN](../protocol/apogee-fln.html).
